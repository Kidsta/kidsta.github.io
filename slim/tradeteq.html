Tradeteq

Role: Senior Product Designer — Design system & critical flows

Making multiple products feel like one

2023 — 2024

TL;DR

Tradeteq had strong fintech capability but a fragmented UI across products. I audited legacy patterns, built a token-first design system, and rolled it out sprint-by-sprint through Storybook. Then I applied it to critical flows—like the due diligence form—reducing completion time and giving the team a scalable way to design and build consistently.

The Challenge

Tradeteq was mature on the fintech side, but the product surface showed years of incremental change—overlapping components, inconsistent branding, and UI stitched together without a system. There was no single source of truth; designers sometimes handed off masked screenshots rather than reusable specs. My job: bring order, consistency, and a way to scale.

  • Legacy & fragmentation: multiple products, different looks/behaviours.
  • No system: duplicated “primary buttons,” overlapping shades, ad-hoc spacing.
  • Key flow pain: long, single-page forms copied from paper; no conditional logic.

Before — mismatched buttons, spacing, and color usage across products.

The Approach

I started with a quick audit to see what was used (and what wasn’t), then built a roadmap: tokens first, core components next, and a steady rollout through Storybook with clear deprecation notes. I paired with the lead FE on naming, states, and usage so design decisions mapped cleanly to code.

  • Audit → roadmap: inventory of components; identified overlaps/gaps.
  • Token-first: color, type, spacing, radii—one shared language for all products.
  • Ship core, then layer: buttons/inputs/tables first; patterns after.
  • Storybook as source: document usage; mark legacy for deprecation.
  • Hands-on pairing: align on states, names, and do/don’t examples.

Design tokens — color ramps, typography scale, and spacing grid.

Core components — buttons, inputs, tables with hover/focus/error states.

The Process

I designed ~80% of the system directly and guided a mid-level designer on the rest. Each sprint introduced a small set of tokens/components; we added them to Storybook, flagged legacy variants, and gave teams a clear migration path. Designers moved from image-based handoffs to system components, and devs could focus on logic, not restyling.

  • Design & guide: I handled most of the system; mentored on the remainder.
  • Review cadence: sprint-by-sprint additions, plus deprecation guidance.
  • System in practice: applied the DS to a critical flow to prove value.

Storybook — usage notes, states, and migration tips for legacy UI.

Critical Flow — Due Diligence

The due diligence form was originally a 1:1 copy of the paper version—everything on one page, no conditional logic, unclear requirements. I kept the familiar ordering to respect offline expectations, but restructured it into steps with up-front document guidance and inline validation. The goal: fewer errors, faster completion, less cognitive load.

  • Familiar order, better structure: same sequence, sectioned steps.
  • Conditional fields: only show “Y” when “X” applies.
  • Upfront guidance: checklist of required docs before users start.
  • Inline validation: fix errors in context; fewer retries.

Before — long single page, no guidance, no conditional logic.

After — sectioned flow, document checklist, conditional fields, inline errors.

Constraints

Design maturity was low: spacing, color, and responsive issues were visible but unfixed; shipping often prioritized “it works” over “it works well.” Direct user access was limited; most validation came via BAs, with engineers lacking full product context. I leaned on fintech experience, quick prototypes, and clear documentation to move things forward without slowing delivery.

Outcome

  • System adoption: tokens and core components added to Storybook; legacy UI flagged for migration.
  • Faster, clearer flow: the due diligence form moved from ~25 min to ~10 min with fewer errors.
  • Better handoffs: designers built with components; devs focused on logic instead of UI surgery.
  • Foundation to scale: a long-term path to consistent, white-label-ready products.

In use — early product screens applying tokens and components.
